# Roadmaps

## Overview

This directory contains learning roadmaps, skill progression guides, technology learning paths, and structured development plans for computing students, developers, researchers, and technology enthusiasts.

Its purpose is to help community members understand the knowledge, skills, tools, and practical experience needed to progress from beginner to intermediate and advanced levels in different areas of computing.

Roadmaps may cover individual technologies, broader technical disciplines, professional development, project-building skills, and interdisciplinary learning paths. They should explain recommended learning sequences, relevant prerequisites, practical activities, and ways to evaluate progress.

Unlike the Learning Resources directory, which provides materials for studying specific subjects, this directory focuses on **what to learn, in what order, why it matters, and how to progress**. Roadmaps should link to suitable learning resources, practical exercises, projects, and relevant opportunities wherever possible.

## Roadmap Categories

Roadmaps may be organized into the following categories:

- **Programming Roadmaps:** Learning paths for programming fundamentals, problem-solving, algorithms, data structures, and individual programming languages.
- **Web Development Roadmaps:** Progression paths covering frontend, backend, full-stack development, web architecture, testing, and deployment.
- **Software Engineering Roadmaps:** Learning sequences for requirements engineering, system design, architecture, testing, version control, collaboration, and software maintenance.
- **Networking Roadmaps:** Paths covering networking fundamentals, protocols, routing, switching, network services, troubleshooting, and advanced networking concepts.
- **Operating Systems Roadmaps:** Learning paths for operating system fundamentals, Linux and Windows environments, system administration, processes, permissions, and internals.
- **Cybersecurity Roadmaps:** Paths covering foundational computing knowledge, networking, operating systems, security principles, defensive security, authorized testing, and specialized security disciplines.
- **Cloud Computing and DevOps Roadmaps:** Learning sequences covering cloud fundamentals, infrastructure, automation, containers, deployment, monitoring, and reliability.
- **Data Science and Artificial Intelligence Roadmaps:** Paths covering programming, mathematics, statistics, data handling, machine learning, and practical AI development.
- **Database and Data Engineering Roadmaps:** Progression through data modeling, SQL, database administration, performance, integration, and data pipelines.
- **Mobile Application Development Roadmaps:** Learning paths covering mobile programming, interface design, application architecture, testing, and deployment.
- **UI/UX and Digital Design Roadmaps:** Paths covering design principles, user research, accessibility, prototyping, interface design, and usability evaluation.
- **Research and Academic Development Roadmaps:** Guides for technical reading, research methods, experimental design, academic writing, reproducibility, and research communication.
- **Open-Source Contribution Roadmaps:** Progression from understanding repositories and version control to making contributions, reviewing code, maintaining projects, and collaborating with communities.
- **Professional Development Roadmaps:** Guides for building portfolios, gaining practical experience, preparing for interviews, collaborating professionally, and identifying suitable career directions.
- **Project-Based Learning Roadmaps:** Step-by-step paths that use practical projects to connect multiple computing skills and demonstrate progress.

Additional categories may be introduced as the community grows.

## Roadmap Components

A useful roadmap should provide enough information for learners to understand both the destination and the steps required to reach it.

Where applicable, each roadmap should include:

- **Title:** The name of the roadmap and the skill area it covers.
- **Overview:** The purpose of the roadmap and the intended learning outcome.
- **Target Audience:** Beginners, intermediate learners, advanced practitioners, or another clearly defined audience.
- **Prerequisites:** Knowledge or skills learners should have before starting.
- **Learning Stages:** A logical progression from foundational concepts to more advanced topics.
- **Skills and Topics:** The concepts, techniques, and abilities to develop at each stage.
- **Recommended Resources:** Links to relevant tutorials, documentation, books, courses, or research materials.
- **Practical Exercises:** Activities that help learners apply each stage's concepts.
- **Projects:** Suggested projects that demonstrate practical understanding.
- **Progress Indicators:** Observable outcomes that help learners evaluate their understanding and readiness to advance.
- **Estimated Effort:** Optional time estimates, clearly identified as approximate and dependent on individual circumstances.
- **Related Roadmaps:** Links to complementary learning paths for interdisciplinary development.

Roadmaps should explain dependencies between topics rather than presenting a disconnected list of technologies or tools.

## Learning Progression

Roadmaps should generally organize learning into stages appropriate to their subject.

- **Foundations:** Understand the essential concepts, terminology, tools, and principles of the chosen field.
- **Core Skills:** Develop the fundamental technical abilities needed to perform common tasks.
- **Practical Application:** Apply knowledge through exercises, experiments, and small projects.
- **Intermediate Development:** Combine multiple concepts, solve less structured problems, and work with more realistic systems.
- **Advanced Specialization:** Explore complex topics, specialized tools, architecture, optimization, research, or advanced techniques.
- **Real-World Practice:** Contribute to projects, collaborate with others, solve practical problems, and document results.
- **Continuous Development:** Keep learning as technologies, standards, tools, and professional practices evolve.

Not every roadmap needs all these stages. Contributors should adapt the progression to the subject, the learner's starting point, and the intended outcome.

## Exploring the Directory

Members can explore roadmaps according to their current abilities, interests, and goals.

- **Beginners:** Choose a roadmap that explains prerequisites and starts with foundational knowledge rather than assuming previous experience.
- **Learners Building Specific Skills:** Follow a roadmap for a particular technology or technical discipline.
- **Project Developers:** Use roadmaps to identify missing knowledge and connect skills needed to complete a project.
- **Interdisciplinary Learners:** Combine complementary roadmaps, such as programming with databases or networking with cybersecurity.
- **Advanced Learners:** Identify specialized topics, research directions, and complex practical projects.
- **Students Preparing for Professional Work:** Use roadmaps to organize practical experience, portfolio development, collaboration, and technical growth.

Learners should treat roadmaps as adaptable guides rather than rigid rules. Learning speed differs between individuals, and some subjects require revisiting earlier concepts as new knowledge develops.

## Roadmap Contribution

Students, lecturers, developers, researchers, and other community members are encouraged to contribute useful roadmaps and improve existing ones.

Contributions may include original learning paths, skill progression diagrams, technology-specific guides, project-based learning sequences, and carefully curated links to external roadmaps.

When contributing a roadmap:

1. Define its purpose and intended learning outcome.
2. Identify the target audience and required prerequisites.
3. Organize topics into a logical sequence.
4. Explain why each stage matters and how it connects to later stages.
5. Include reliable learning resources for important topics.
6. Suggest exercises or projects that demonstrate practical understanding.
7. Provide observable outcomes that help learners assess their progress.
8. Cite external sources and respect applicable copyright and licensing conditions.
9. Review links and update recommendations when technologies or learning requirements change.

Contributors should follow the repository's existing directory structure, naming conventions, and contribution instructions.

## Copyright and Licensing

Respect the intellectual property rights of roadmap authors, educators, publishers, organizations, and other content owners.

- Submit original roadmaps or materials that you have the right to share.
- Attribute external roadmaps, diagrams, and learning resources appropriately.
- Do not copy third-party roadmaps in full unless their licenses or permissions allow redistribution.
- Link to original resources when copying or redistribution is not permitted or is unclear.
- Preserve applicable copyright notices and license information.
- Clearly identify the license of contributed diagrams, code examples, and other reusable materials.

A link to an external resource does not imply that TCOSC owns or endorses its content.

## Contribution Principles

TCOSC encourages roadmaps that are:

- **Structured:** Organized into meaningful stages with clear dependencies.
- **Practical:** Connected to exercises, projects, and observable learning outcomes.
- **Accessible:** Understandable to the intended audience and explicit about prerequisites.
- **Relevant:** Focused on useful computing skills and realistic learning goals.
- **Adaptable:** Allowing learners to adjust their pace and explore alternative approaches.
- **Reliable:** Supported by credible resources and technically sound recommendations.
- **Current:** Reviewed periodically as technologies, standards, and practices change.
- **Collaborative:** Open to feedback, corrections, and improvements from the community.

The objective is to help learners make informed decisions about what to learn next and develop the skills needed to apply computing knowledge effectively.

Through community-maintained roadmaps, members can help one another navigate computing disciplines, build practical skills, and progress toward meaningful academic, technical, and professional goals.
