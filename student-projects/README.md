# Student Projects

Welcome to the **Tanzania Computing Open Source Community (TCOSC) Student Projects** repository.

This section brings together computing projects developed by students and other community members across universities, colleges, and learning institutions in Tanzania. It provides a place to share work, learn from others, collaborate, improve technical skills, and contribute to open-source software.

## About

TCOSC Student Projects encourages practical learning through the development, documentation, testing, review, and maintenance of computing projects.

Projects may be created for personal learning, academic assignments, diploma and degree requirements, research, community needs, or real-world problem-solving. Both beginners and experienced contributors are welcome.

## Objectives

- Encourage practical application of computing knowledge.
- Promote collaboration among students from different institutions.
- Support open-source software development and knowledge sharing.
- Provide opportunities to learn from existing projects.
- Encourage responsible research, documentation, and software engineering practices.
- Help students build practical experience and demonstrable portfolios.
- Support projects beyond graduation through collaboration and maintenance.
- Connect students, developers, researchers, educators, and other computing enthusiasts.

## Areas of Contribution

Projects may cover any computing-related field, including:

- **Software Development:** desktop applications, enterprise systems, utilities, and software tools.
- **Web Development:** websites, web applications, APIs, and backend systems.
- **Mobile Development:** Android, iOS, and cross-platform applications.
- **Programming:** algorithms, data structures, programming exercises, libraries, and developer tools.
- **Networking and Infrastructure:** network tools, network monitoring, infrastructure management, and network automation.
- **Cybersecurity:** defensive security tools, security research, vulnerability assessment, digital forensics, and security awareness.
- **Databases:** database applications, data management, administration, and optimization.
- **Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning:** intelligent applications, models, automation, and experimentation.
- **Data Science and Analytics:** data processing, visualization, statistical analysis, and reporting.
- **Cloud Computing and DevOps:** deployment, automation, containers, CI/CD, and cloud infrastructure.
- **Operating Systems and Virtualization:** system utilities, Linux tools, virtualization, and operating-system experiments.
- **Embedded Systems and IoT:** microcontroller projects, connected devices, sensors, and automation.
- **UI/UX and Digital Design:** interface design, accessibility, prototypes, and user-experience improvements.
- **Computing Research:** experiments, technical investigations, reproducible studies, and research software.
- **Interdisciplinary Projects:** computing solutions addressing challenges in education, agriculture, healthcare, business, environment, and other sectors.

These areas are not restrictive. Projects combining multiple computing disciplines are encouraged.

## Who Can Contribute?

TCOSC welcomes contributions from:

- University and college students.
- Diploma, undergraduate, and postgraduate students.
- Recent graduates and independent learners.
- Software developers and computing professionals.
- Researchers and educators.
- Open-source contributors and project maintainers.

Contributors from different institutions and experience levels are encouraged to learn and work together.

## Types of Projects

Contributions may include:

- New open-source applications and systems.
- Student and final-year projects.
- Research prototypes and experimental implementations.
- Programming exercises and learning projects.
- Existing projects seeking contributors.
- Improvements, bug fixes, testing, and documentation.
- Tools that address practical challenges in Tanzania and beyond.

Projects do not need to be complete before they can be shared. Work in progress is welcome when its current status and limitations are clearly explained.

## Project Documentation

Every project should include documentation appropriate to its size and maturity. Contributors should explain:

- The project's name and purpose.
- The problem it aims to solve.
- Its main features and current development status.
- The technologies and tools used.
- Installation, configuration, and usage instructions where applicable.
- How others can contribute or report issues.
- Known limitations and planned improvements.
- Licensing information and acknowledgements where relevant.

Documentation should help another person understand the project and, where possible, run, test, or improve it.

## Contribution Guidelines

Before contributing, review the applicable repository guidelines and the project's existing documentation.

Contributors are expected to:

1. Understand the project's purpose and current state.
2. Discuss significant changes with the maintainers when appropriate.
3. Make changes that are relevant, understandable, and testable.
4. Document important functionality and configuration.
5. Test changes before submitting them.
6. Explain the purpose of proposed contributions.
7. Respond constructively to review feedback.
8. Respect project ownership, licensing, and community standards.

Contributions may include source code, documentation, tests, translations, issue reports, design improvements, research notes, and maintenance work.

## Collaboration and Learning

TCOSC encourages contributors to learn together rather than work in isolation.

Community members can help by explaining concepts, reviewing code, identifying bugs, testing applications, improving documentation, suggesting features, and mentoring newer contributors.

Beginners are encouraged to ask questions, start with manageable tasks, and learn from existing projects. Experienced contributors are encouraged to share knowledge and help others develop their skills.

## Student and Research Projects

Students may contribute academic and research-related projects, including final-year projects, provided they have the right to share the material.

Contributors must follow their institution's academic integrity requirements, acknowledge collaborators and sources, and respect applicable intellectual property rights. Sharing a project through TCOSC does not replace institutional submission or approval requirements.

Research contributions should describe their methods, evidence, limitations, and results accurately. Experimental results must not be fabricated or presented as verified when they have not been tested.

## Security, Privacy, and Responsible Development

Projects must be developed and shared responsibly.

- Do not commit passwords, API keys, access tokens, private keys, or other secrets.
- Do not publish personal or confidential information without proper authorization.
- Security testing must be conducted only on systems for which the contributor has permission.
- Do not include malicious code or instructions intended to harm others.
- Explain security limitations and known risks where relevant.
- Report sensitive vulnerabilities privately through the appropriate project or repository reporting channel.

Contributors are responsible for ensuring that their submissions comply with applicable laws, licences, and community policies.

## Open Source and Licensing

Contributors should clearly identify the licence governing their projects and ensure they have permission to publish the code, documentation, datasets, and other included materials.

Do not assume that all materials found online are free to redistribute. Credit original authors and comply with the terms of third-party dependencies and resources.

Where a project has no licence, its reuse and redistribution permissions may be unclear. Contributors should address licensing before inviting others to reuse or distribute the work.

## Quality and Maintenance

TCOSC values understandable, maintainable, and useful projects over unnecessary complexity.

Contributors should aim for readable code, clear documentation, appropriate testing, and honest descriptions of project maturity. Projects may evolve through review, refactoring, issue resolution, and community collaboration.

When a contributor becomes unavailable or graduates, the community should be encouraged to preserve useful knowledge and, where the project owner permits, help find maintainers to continue the work.

## Community Conduct

All contributors must treat other community members with respect, regardless of institution, background, experience, or technical ability.

Constructive discussion, honest feedback, responsible collaboration, and willingness to learn are essential. Harassment, discrimination, plagiarism, deliberate disruption, and other unacceptable behaviour are not permitted.

Please follow the applicable TCOSC community standards and repository policies.

## Our Learning and Contribution Principles

TCOSC promotes a continuous cycle of practical learning and community contribution:

**LEARN → PRACTICE → BUILD → SHARE → COLLABORATE → CONTRIBUTE → REVIEW → MAINTAIN → MENTOR**

Every project can become an opportunity for its creator and other community members to learn, improve, and contribute to the wider computing community.
